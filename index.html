Transistor Physics from 4 K to 1000 K

We measure High Electron Mobility Transistors (HEMTs) and other Wide-Bandgap Semiconductor Devices from cryogenic to high temperature, uncover the physics that limits them, and build compact models for RF, power, space and quantum electronics.

About Us

Led by Dr. Taewoo Kim in the Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering at Texas Tech University, the Advanced Semiconductor Device Laboratory studies how carrier transport, trapping and self-heating govern the performance and stability of Wide-Bandgap Semiconductor Devices, especially High Electron Mobility Transistors (HEMTs) built on GaN, InGaAs and related materials.

We combine temperature-dependent DC, pulsed, noise and 67 GHz RF measurements with physics-based parameter extraction and ASM-HEMT compact modeling, and we study how radiation changes device behavior for space and defense electronics.
